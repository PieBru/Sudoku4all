First, breathe

Sudoku looks scary. It is not. These pages never time you. They never count mistakes. Mistakes are normal. Everyone makes them.

The real game has a clock. It only reports your time. It never rushes you. The game counts mistakes. It never punishes you. Undo always rescues you. Here you cannot fail. Really.

What is this game?

A Sudoku is a big square. Lines across are rows. Lines down are columns. Each small square is a cell. Fat lines cut big boxes.

One rule fills them all. Each row holds every fruit once. Each column too. Each box too. That is the whole game!

Grown-ups: these small grids are real Sudoku in miniature — same rules, friendlier size. Early wins matter more than speed, so nothing here counts, times, or judges.

Play with fruit

Tap a square. Tap a fruit. Wrong fruit? It wiggles away. Nothing bad happens. Fill every square to win.

Now with numbers

Numbers now. Same game. Each row, column and box holds 1 to 4. Grown squares stay locked. Fill the rest.

A bit bigger

Six by six now. Still friendly. Rows and columns hold 1 to 6. Boxes are two rows tall. Take your time.

Grown-ups: the number keys are size-aware — 1 to 4 on the small boards, 1 to 6 here. Wrong picks only wiggle; correct picks stay.

The big one

The big board works the same. Rows, columns and boxes. Just nine of each. You are ready when the little ones feel easy.

Try it

Finish one tiny board alone. Then finish a second. Then finish the six by six. Then peek at the big game. Easy level is your friend.

Checkpoint

Can you do these three things?

  1. Say what a row is.
  2. Say what a box is.
  3. Finish a tiny board alone.

You graduate when both boards fall without help.
